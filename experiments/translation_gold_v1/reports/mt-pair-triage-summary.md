# MT pair candidate triage (Phase 1)

Status: silver / AI-triaged, not independent gold.

Source: `D:\Coding\thesis\curated_research_dataset\kapampangan_mt_pld_pam_fil_curated.csv`
(250 rows), copied byte-for-byte into `resources/` (see
`resources/provenance-manifest.json`). The original file at its external
path was never modified.

## Why this dataset needed triage, not direct use

Per the 2026-08-25 dataset-audit entry in `AGENT_CONTEXT.md`, these 250 rows
were generated by cross-matching PAM and FIL entries within the same PLD
elicitation-domain file/prompt position, not by aligning real translations
-- confirmed directly (row 1 pairs "Cayucut", a tailbone/coccyx gloss, with
"balunbalunan", Filipino for gizzard). `dataset_role` already says
`pld_prompt_aligned_mt_candidates` / `validation_status:
needs_alignment_review` for every row.

## Method

Every row was read directly against a stated, written rubric -- not a
single opaque pass/fail judgment -- and the reasoning for each score was
recorded (`reports/mt-pair-rubric-scores.tsv`, one row per candidate, joined
against the source CSV by `triage_mt_pairs.py`):

| Score | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 3 | `correct_translation` -- a clear, verifiable correct translation. |
| 2 | `plausible_partial_match` -- meaningful overlap (shared formulaic structure, shared core concept, or same topic with unverified exact detail) without being a confirmed correct translation. |
| 1 | `same_domain_wrong_specific` -- both texts belong to the same elicitation domain (kinship, time, greetings, medical, economic news, proverbs, etc.) but do not mean the same thing. |
| 0 | `no_correspondence` -- no plausible semantic connection at all. |

A separate, unscored category -- `dictionary_gloss_pair` (rows 42-50) --
covers homonym/homograph dictionary illustration entries (two senses of one
Kapampangan word, each defined) that are not sentence/phrase translation
pairs at all, regardless of their paired target text.

## User spot-check corrections (2026-08-25)

The user reviewed a stratified sample and corrected two rows this session's
own AI scoring got wrong -- both errors of the same kind: confident-sounding
but incorrect claims about what a Kapampangan or Filipino word means,
stated without checking:

- **Row 10** (`Bucung-bucung` / `galáng-galangán`) was originally scored
  3/`correct_translation` on the claim that `galang-galangan` is Tagalog for
  "ankle." It is not: `galáng-galangán` denotes the *wrist* in Filipino
  (Filipino for ankle is `bukung-bukong`), so this pairs the Kapampangan
  word for ankle with the Filipino word for a different joint. Corrected to
  1/`same_domain_wrong_specific`.
- **Row 160** (`Nucarin ya ing seli cung lapis nandin?` / `Nakanino ang susi
  ng bahay?`) was originally scored 2/`plausible_partial_match` on the claim
  that PAM `seli` is a cognate of Filipino `susi` ("key"). It is not: `seli`
  here means "bought" (from root `sali`), and the sentence means "Where is
  the pencil I bought earlier?" -- unrelated to the FIL target's question
  about a house key. Corrected to 0/`no_correspondence`.

Both corrections are recorded directly in
`reports/mt-pair-rubric-scores.tsv` (search for "USER-CORRECTED") rather
than silently edited, so the original AI judgment and the correction both
stay visible in the audit trail.

## The more important finding: this is not just noise, it's shuffled signal

Reading every row directly (rather than scoring in isolation) surfaced a
pattern the per-row rubric alone would have missed: in several later blocks
of this dataset (weather, greetings, questions, economic news, proverbs),
the **same PAM source sentence recurs at multiple rows**, each time paired
with a **different** FIL target -- and in a concrete, verifiable number of
cases, one of those "wrong" pairings is actually the *correct* translation
of a source sentence sitting at a **different row**, whose own target is
also wrong. Twelve rows carry an explicit note documenting this (see the
`note` column in `reports/mt-pair-triage.csv`, or search it for "true match"
/ "shift"); the clearest, most independently verifiable examples:

- **Rows 89-92 (weather):** row 89's source ("Bugsu ning angin", gust of
  wind) is paired with "halumigmig" (humidity) -- wrong. Row 90's source
  ("Maialumigmig", humid) is paired with "biglaang pagbaha" (sudden
  flooding) -- also wrong. But row 91 correctly pairs "Bugsu ning angin"
  with "bugso ng hangin" (gust of wind, exact match). The natural reading:
  "halumigmig" (row 89's wrong target) is very plausibly the *true* target
  for row 90's source ("Maialumigmig"/humid) -- i.e. this four-row block is
  off by one position, not randomly scrambled.
- **Rows 206/207/233 (economic news):** row 206's source ("Planu ning Banco
  Central ning Filipinas a ilaco king circulacion ing tantiang limampulung
  bilyung pesus...") is a near word-for-word match for the FIL text that
  instead sits at row 233 ("Plano ng Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas na alisin
  sa sirkulasyon ang tinatayang limampung bilyong pisong...") -- but row
  233's own source is a different sentence entirely (shared with rows
  204/227, about a company relocating to Subic).
- **Rows 213/225:** row 213's source ("Maingat ngening pagaralan ning local
  a capamalan ning lungsud ning Menila...") is a near word-for-word match
  for the FIL text sitting at row 225 ("Masusing pinag-aaralan ngayon ng
  lokal na pamahalaan ng lungsod ng Manila... baywalk sa kahabaan ng Roxas
  Blvd."), again with each row's *own* target being wrong.
- **Rows 236/245/247/249 and 242/243/246/250 (proverbs):** the well-known
  proverb "Ang hindi marunong lumingon sa pinanggalingan ay hindi
  makararating sa paroroonan" (roughly, "one who forgets where they came
  from won't reach their destination") appears as the FIL target at four
  different rows (236, 245, 247, 249), each time paired with a different,
  non-matching Kapampangan source -- strong evidence its true PAM source
  (most plausibly row 236's, which is itself a close paraphrase of the same
  idea) exists in this dataset but not at any row where it's actually
  paired correctly. Separately, row 246's source ("Acu ing mitanam, ing
  binayu at siniasat, saca niang melutu aliua ya ing mengan" -- I planted,
  pounded, and inspected it, but someone else ate it once cooked) is a
  close match for the FIL text sitting at row 243 ("Ako ang nagbayo, ako
  ang nagsaing, saka ng maluto'y iba ang kumain"), and row 250's source is
  a close match for the FIL text sitting at row 242.

**Why this matters for Phase 2**: this dataset's PAM and FIL columns
plausibly originate from two lists of the *same* underlying sentences that
became desynchronized (shifted and/or locally shuffled) during whatever
process produced this CSV, rather than being unrelated text cross-matched
purely at random. That means a modest amount of genuine, recoverable
parallel signal likely exists in the 250 rows beyond the 9 pairs already
confirmed correct here -- but recovering it needs a real
similarity/alignment pass (e.g. embedding-based nearest-neighbor matching
of each PAM source against the full FIL target pool, not just its own row's
target), which is exactly the "real extraction/alignment pipeline" work
already scoped for Phase 2, not a fix to attempt inside this triage pass.
This triage does not attempt that realignment; it only documents the
evidence found while scoring, so Phase 2 does not have to rediscover it.

## What this triage does not establish

- **Not gold.** Even the 9 distinct tier-A pairs are this session's own
  reading, not native-speaker-verified. They should still be spot-checked.
- Tier B (`plausible_partial_match`) is a judgment call about whether two
  texts are "close enough to be worth a second look," not a confirmed
  translation -- several of these (e.g. rows 100, 185: correct greeting
  formula, wrong time-of-day) are template matches with one specific word
  wrong, which may or may not be useful depending on downstream tolerance.
- Tier C (`same_domain_wrong_specific`) rows are not simply useless: they
  confirm the dataset's elicitation-domain structure (kinship terms paired
  with kinship terms, weather with weather, etc.), which is exactly the
  structure that makes the shuffle-realignment approach above plausible.
- No claim is made about the *quality* of the underlying PLD source data
  itself, or about which specific realignment (if attempted) would recover
  correct pairs -- only that the block-recurring-target pattern is real and
  independently checkable from the note column.
